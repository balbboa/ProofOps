## 2. Per-candidate notes

### Part 1: the three R3 anomalies

**1. Real-estate transaction coordination.** *Paid:* the per-file market is solid and stable in 2026: $300–500/file flat, national average $350–450 ([Rebillion](https://rebillion.ai/blog/2026/04/13/transaction-coordinator-cost/)); Empower charges $400/file, or $500 in California ([Empower](https://www.empowertransactions.com/how-much-does-a-transaction-coordinator-cost/)). TC salary averages $58k ([Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/career/transaction-coordinator/salaries)). *What the human does:* in the morning, reviews every file for deadlines and triages overnight email from lenders and title. Mid-morning, follows up on inspections, documents and title issues. In the afternoon, checks document completeness and prepares amendments. All day, relays messages between parties ([Freedom RES](https://www.freedom-res.com/what-does-a-transaction-coordinator-do/)). "Phone calls, emails, and regular follow-ups with mortgage lenders, home inspectors, title company staff, surveyors, and appraisers take up significant time" ([Indeed career advice](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/finding-a-job/transaction-coordinator)). *Software could do:* the timeline, deadline reminders, document checklists, email drafts (ListedKit already does this at $14.99). *Hours:* TCs save agents 8–20 h per file ([AgentUp](https://www.agentup.com/learn/how-many-hours-can-you-save-with-a-transaction-coordinator)). One TC with automation handles 20–30 files a month ([Avenue](https://www.avetransactions.com/blog/maximizing-efficiency-determining-the-transaction-load-for-a-transaction-coordinator)), so about 5–8 h per file even with software. *Capacity:* 43 h ≈ 5–8 files a month ≈ $2,000–3,200. It could fit on paper, but only as a TC operating by phone. *K12-relaxed:* not licensed in most states if purely clerical ([AgentUp legal](https://www.agentup.com/blog/the-legal-side-of-real-estate-transaction-coordination)). **But third-party phone calls are core and unavoidable**, and closing deadlines need same-day response in US hours. **KILL.** Why buyers ignore ListedKit: the part they pay for is chasing people, which the $15 tool cannot do. That supports the R3 anomaly, but the chasing happens by phone.

**1b. Listing coordination.** Pure paperwork (MLS input, listing checklist). Yet the productised DFY price has collapsed to $15/listing ([Homelystic](https://homelystic.com/listing-coordinator)). **KILL.** This matters for the segment finding: once a TC sub-task is separable and async, someone productises it at almost nothing.

**2. Insurance-agency service VAs (COI issuance, renewals, endorsements).** Agency VA's $3,449/mo "Trained Service Teams" explicitly include "Phone support" ([agencyva.com/pricing](https://www.agencyva.com/pricing)). Offshore insurance VAs run $8–18/h ([Sonant](https://www.sonant.ai/blog/insurance-virtual-assistant-cost)). Per-certificate economics: a standard COI takes 10 min, outsourced at $3–7 per certificate, with a 30-minute turnaround SLA, and outsourcing only pays above 15–20 certificates a day *(vendor claim)* ([Clermont](https://clermontglobal.com/blog/coi-processing-cost-comparison.html)). *Why humans despite free Certificial:* the certificate is triggered by an inbound request (email or phone from the insured) and needs an agency-side decision about additional-insured wording, all inside the AMS. **KILL:** SLA and time-zone mismatch, AMS lock-in (K13), phone, a $3–7 floor, and licensed handling of endorsements and coverage questions.

**3. Amazon FBA reimbursement managers.** Contingency pricing (GETIDA 25%, others 12–18%) ([Eva](https://eva.guru/blog/amazon-fba-reimbursement/)). Amazon's policy changes (60-day window from Oct 2024; manufacturing-cost basis from Mar 2025) cut average recovery from about $18.50 to $6.20 per unit, per the search snippet ([Seller Labs](https://www.sellerlabs.com/blog/amazon-2025-fba-reimbursement-policy-change/), [Nova](https://www.novadata.io/resources/news/amazon-reimbursement-policy-manufacturing-cost)). A fixed €100–500 fee competes with "pay only if recovered". Case filing happens inside Seller Central (K13). **KILL**, now stronger than in R3.

### Part 1: analogues

**4. Mortgage loan processing.** Per-file prices are high ($695–1,595) ([Empower](https://www.empowertransactions.com/contract-processor-vs-virtual-assistant/)), but independent processors need a loan-originator licence ([WA DFI](https://dfi.wa.gov/loan-originators/loan-processors-frequently-asked-questions)), and the work involves borrower, appraiser and title calls. **KILL** (licensed, phone).

**5. PM lease renewals.** The work is tracking renewal dates, drafting offers and notices, and filing. But offshore renewal VAs cost $9–13/h ([assistworld](https://assistworld.com/property-management-virtual-assistant-company/)), and renewals involve rent negotiation with tenants. **KILL.**

**6. Freight-broker back office.** Work is per load, every day: POD chase, carrier invoice audit, billing, AR ([ClearLane](https://getclearlane.com/solutions/freight-brokers/)). POD chasing and collections are phone-heavy. Carrier onboarding is an established category (R2). **KILL.**

**7. ISNetworld/Avetta upkeep.** Program writing is one-off ($500–2,500 per program; $149 template alternative) ([CrewCompliance](https://crewcompliance.org/isnetworld-ravs-safety-programs/)). Ongoing work is quarterly OSHA hours and annual 300A ([Ironwood](https://www.ironwoodbc.com/services/isnetworld-compliance/)). **KILL** (K15, K1).

**8. DFY COI tracking for small GCs/PMs:** see §3.

**9. DFY certified payroll:** see §3.

**10. Sub pay applications.** Monthly G702/G703 plus lien waivers plus a compliance package per project. One missing waiver holds a billing cycle 2–4 weeks ([Built](https://getbuilt.com/blog/aia-g702-pay-application/), snippet). Textura charges subs 0.22% of contract value (max $3,750) to submit ([Law Insider clause](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/texturatm-construction-payment-management-system), snippet). No standalone DFY price found. **UNCERTAIN (weak):** worth testing only as an add-on to #9 for the same buyer.

**11. GSA Schedule maintenance.** Retainers $1,250–2,500/mo ([Winvale](https://info.winvale.com/blog/how-much-does-gsa-schedule-maintenance-cost)). The generatable parts (quarterly 72A sales report, catalog/SIP upload) are quarterly or occasional. The monthly retainer pays for modifications and pricing talks with the contracting officer. **KILL** (K15 + K12 negotiation). Low-value remnant lead: a "GSA reporting + catalog upkeep" plan for tiny schedule holders. There is no evidence anyone pays for that alone.

**12–14. Productised DFY already at or below our band:** DQ files at $30/driver/mo ([DotFleet](https://www.dotfleetcompliance.com/)); Intrastat at €89/mo ([DutyPay](https://www.dutypay.eu/en-US/service/intrastat-service/)); STR lodging tax at $27/property/mo (MyLodgeTax, snippet). **KILL** each (K1 reinterpreted: the competitor *is* a cheap productised human+software service).

## 3. Uncertain candidates: deep-dive (R4's four questions)

### #9 DFY certified payroll for small public-works subcontractors (strongest)

1. **Who pays a human today, how much, how often?**
   - Mid-size contractors employ certified payroll specialists at $22–38/h ([ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Certified-Payroll)).
   - Managed services exist: Points North prepares and submits to DIR and LCPtracker ([Points North](https://www.points-north.com/managed-service)); eBacon has a managed tier; regional construction bookkeepers do it ([Devine Consulting](https://devineconsultingllc.com/benefits-of-outsourcing-certified-payroll-for-construction-businesses/)).
   - Price evidence: one example of ~$1,200/mo ($750 base + $25/report) for 15 workers on 3 projects. **This comes from a competitor's page, so treat it as a claim** ([CertifiedPayrollPro](https://www.certifiedpayrollpro.com/certified-payroll-services)).
   - A contractor quoted on [davisbaconrates.com](https://davisbaconrates.com/certified-payroll-software) hired "a payroll consultant" because it "saves me ALOT of time".
   - Frequency: **weekly** per active project. Federal filings go on WH-347; the new form is mandatory from Oct 2026 ([Points North](https://www.points-north.com/trends-and-insights/still-using-the-old-wh-347-deadline-is-september-2026)). In California, *every* contractor and sub on *every* public-works job must file through DIR eCPR, at least monthly and usually weekly ([DIR](https://www.dir.ca.gov/public-works/certified-payroll-reporting.html), snippet).
2. **Why not the cheap tool?**
   - Self-serve tools are cheap: LCPcertified $12/report or $145/mo for 5 projects ([LCPtracker](https://lcptracker.com/solutions/lcpcertified/)); CertifiedPayrollPro $49; LeaveSheet $19.
   - The pain is the setup and the rules, not the form: wage determinations, classifications, fringe calculations, the new form's fringe breakdown, and correction cycles. Gusto produces no WH-347 ([CertifiedPayrollPro/Gusto](https://www.certifiedpayrollpro.com/blog/certified-payroll-with-gusto-wh347-guide)). One missed fringe cost $13,508 in back wages ([davisbaconrates](https://davisbaconrates.com/certified-payroll-software)).
   - **Adversarial point:** LCPtracker says a sub with fewer than 15 employees spends about **30 min/week** entering a report ([ND DOT FAQ](https://www.dot.nd.gov/sites/default/files/documents/civil-rights/FAQ%20LCPtracker%207%20mar%202024.pdf), snippet). Another source says "2–3 hours every Friday" for QuickBooks users. If 30 minutes is typical, the in-house cost is only about $60–150/mo, which weakens a €250+ price.
3. **Human time and capacity.**
   - A typical customer: 2 active projects, ~10 workers, ~9 reports/month.
   - With payroll export → validation → XML/CSV import, about 10 min per report ≈ 1.5 h. Add 0.5–1 h for corrections and agency queries, plus 1–2 h per new project for wage-determination setup (amortised ~0.5 h). **≈3–4 h/month.**
   - At €300/mo: 4 customers = €1,200 MRR, ~14 h. At €200/mo: 5 customers = €1,000, ~18 h. **Fits the 43 h cap.**
   - Leverage: 2–3 h/week manual → ~1 h/week, so it passes. Against the "30 min/week" figure it passes only on the setup/exception share.
4. **Self-serve purchase?** Plausible: fixed price per active project per month, with checkout. The trigger is acute and searchable ("first prevailing wage job", "certified payroll service"). Customer inputs are a payroll export plus the contract/wage determination, and the customer signs the Statement of Compliance. SEO competition is heavy (many vendor blogs).

**Open risks:** US-only; wage-classification errors carry back-wage exposure (**E&O insurance needed**); churn when public projects end (partial K15); payroll data includes worker IDs (last-4 SSN on the new form). The **decisive evidence gap** is a published fixed price from a small-sub DFY provider, or buyer posts paying $200+/mo.

### #8 DFY COI tracking for small GCs and property managers (60–150 vendors)

1. **Who pays, how much?**
   - Mid-size GCs employ coordinators ($62–74k, snippet).
   - Full-service tracking exists but has **a $10k/yr minimum** (BCS $17.80/vendor/yr; CertFocus $13–29/vendor/yr) ([getbcs](https://www.getbcs.com/pricing-and-plans), [Vertikal](https://www.vertikalrms.com/article/how-much-does-coi-tracking-software-cost-2026-pricing-guide/)). myCOI Concierge is quote-only, "$500+/mo" per [COI File](https://coifile.com/coi-tracking/best-software/).
   - Construction VAs from $15/h list COI tracking ([Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/construction-virtual-assistant/)).
   - Economic stakes: expired-COI stoppages; workers'-comp audit reclassification of uninsured subs' payments ([ExpirationReminder](https://www.expirationreminder.com/blog/cost-of-missing-coi-renewal-case-studies), vendor blog).
2. **Why not the cheap tool?** The honest answer is that they often *should* use it. BCS is free up to 25 vendors with automated requests and deficiency notices; COI File is $39–199; TrackMyVendor $39. What those tools don't include is human review of endorsements (additional insured, waiver of subrogation, completed-ops) and ownership of exceptions. The evidence that small buyers skip the tool is only "they use spreadsheets" statements from vendors. **Weak.**
3. **Human time and capacity.**
   - Per customer with 60 subs: ~2.5 policy events per sub per year ≈ 12–13 certificate reviews a month at ~10 min with AI extraction ≈ 2 h.
   - Deficiency emails ≈ 0.5–1 h; monthly compliance report ≈ 0.5 h. **≈3–4 h/month.**
   - €250 × 4–5 customers ≈ €1,000–1,250 MRR, 15–20 h. Fits. Leverage over a manual coordinator is >50%.
4. **Self-serve purchase?** Yes, in principle: price by vendor-count tier, upload a requirements template and vendor list.
- **Blocking questions:**
  - (a) The service must **email third parties** (subs and their agents). R4 hasn't ruled on that.
  - (b) BCS full-service uses **phone** outreach. Billy reports email-only submission rates of ~60% in 30 days ([Billy](https://billyforinsurance.com/property-management/), snippet), so email-only DFY may underperform.
  - (c) Reviewing endorsements is insurance expertise. CertFocus uses credentialed staff (CIC/CPCU). Missed deficiencies create liability claims against the service, close to the R4 "legal liability" kill line.

## 4. Segment finding

1. **R3 pattern #4 is real but mostly sits on the phone.** Buyers do pay humans $300–500/file (TC), $3,449/mo (insurance service teams) and 12–25% contingency (FBA) despite $0–15 tools. When you take apart what the human does all day, the part tools can't do is **live, synchronous chasing of third parties** (lenders, title, carriers, drivers, city clerks, insureds), plus SLAs of 30 minutes to same day. That part breaks R4's no-phone rule and the 10 h/week, Brazil-time-zone capacity. The anomaly does not transfer to a solo async DFY.
2. **When a sub-task is async and separable, it has already been productised cheaply.** Examples: MLS listing input $15/listing, DQ files $30/driver/mo, Intrastat €89/mo, STR lodging tax $27/property/mo, COI issuance $3–7/cert. Productised DFY competitors already sit **at or below** the €100–500 band. This mirrors R3's "pricing squeeze", one level up: tools → productised DFY → humans.
3. **The remaining gap is "managed service with enterprise minimums, cheap self-serve tool below, nothing in between."** Both uncertain survivors (#8 COI, #9 certified payroll) sit in this gap ($10k/yr and ~$1,200/mo managed floors versus $12–49 tools). The gap may exist because small buyers won't pay €200+ for something a $39 tool plus 30 min a week handles. **That is the hypothesis desk research can't settle.**
4. Job postings add little new. Most narrow roles are phone- or portal-bound (TC, permits, warranty, lettings) or offshore-commodity (catalog). Only compliance coordinators and certified payroll specialists have mostly generatable, async duty lists.
