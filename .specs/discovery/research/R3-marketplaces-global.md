## 0. Research constraints (read first; they limit how much weight this file can carry)

- **WebSearch quota was exhausted** (200/200 for the session) before this agent made its first query. **No search-engine queries were run.** All "competition checks" below are direct fetches of vendor pricing pages I already knew by name. That is weaker than the adversarial search the brief asked for ("X software", "X automation", and so on). Missing an even cheaper competitor is possible. Missing *all* competitors is not, since each candidate below already has one priced tool.
- **Fiverr, Upwork (job pages and Project Catalog) and OnlineJobs.ph returned HTTP 403** to direct fetches. I did not work around this. So there is **no Fiverr/Upwork/OnlineJobs.ph evidence** in this file. Contra's services URL returned 404.
- Fetchable sources used: **Freelancer.com** public job category and project pages; **PeoplePerHour (PPH)** public `/services/<keyword>` listing pages; vendor pricing pages.
- **Caveats about the volume signals:**
  - **PPH review counts shown on listing pages belong to the seller, not the offer.** Proof: the "Monthly Generative Engine Optimization reporting" offer is listed with "942 reviews" on the [listing page](https://www.peopleperhour.com/services/monthly-report), but its [offer page](https://www.peopleperhour.com/hourlie/monthly-generative-engine-optimization-visibility-reporting/1087791) says 0 sales. PPH counts therefore show that a seller is active, not that a deliverable sells in volume.
  - **Freelancer "bids" measure supply (freelancers pitching), not demand.** One post = one buyer.
  - Freelancer budgets are posted ranges or average bids, not transaction prices.

## 2. Per-candidate notes (only what the table doesn't already say)

**C1 Monthly QBO/Xero bank reconciliation.** This is the most repeated recurring posting on Freelancer: "Monthly QB Online Reconciliation" (2 bank accounts, month-end summary, ₹750–1,250/hr) ([1](https://www.freelancer.com/projects/accounting/monthly-online-reconciliation)), "Monthly QuickBooks Bank Reconciliation … by the 10th" ($9/hr) and "Ongoing QuickBooks Entry & Reconciliation Support" (22–25 h/month, $6/hr) ([2](https://www.freelancer.com/jobs/bookkeeping/)). *Economics:* at $6–9/hr × 3–25 h/month, buyers spend roughly $20–225/month. That is about our price, not 2–5× it. *Why not software:* the ledger vendor ships the matching engine. What buyers are paying for is someone to accept responsibility for the exceptions (K12). This is ProofOps-shaped (two-system reconciliation), which the founder ruled out anyway.

**C4 UK payroll.** Human labour at $15/run ([10](https://www.peopleperhour.com/services/payroll)) against free government software ([11](https://www.gov.uk/basic-paye-tools)). The buyer is already choosing a human over free software, so the paid value is outsourcing responsibility (K12).

**C8 Product upload.** $60 buys 1,000 simple products from a human ([15](https://www.peopleperhour.com/services/product-upload)). That is a one-time catalogue load. Ongoing catalogue maintenance is covered by Matrixify's scheduled imports ([17](https://apps.shopify.com/excel-export-import)).

**C10 FBA reimbursement.** Strongest *frequency* signal found ("every working day", full-time) ([19](https://www.freelancer.com/projects/amazon/seller-central-reimbursement-case)). The buyer's spend is ≈ $8/hr × ~160 h ≈ $1,280/month at the top of the posted range (my arithmetic; the post says "full-time"). But the market already sells this as a contingency service ([20](https://www.getida.com/pricing)), and the claim must be filed inside Seller Central (K13, UNVERIFIED for API). The posting says the workflow "will rely heavily on ChatGPT", so the buyer already treats it as AI-assisted labour. Killed.

**C12 Export documents.** The documents themselves are commoditised ([24](https://incodocs.com/pricing)). The expensive half of the $25–50/hr role is chasing freight forwarders, brokers and carriers ([23](https://www.freelancer.com/projects/logistics-shipping/international-shipment-coordination)): K12 relationships. This matches R2's logistics result.

**C17 E3 report assembly.** The only niche, non-obvious recurring deliverable found. It still dies. The statute limits who may inspect and sets a 6-year cycle with the first deadline already passed ([32](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=HSC&sectionNum=17973)). The single posting's project page 404s, so there's no further detail. The incumbent report software (HomeGauge/Spectora) is where any templating would live (K9).

## 4. Segment-level finding (why marketplaces are a poor source for this R3 thesis)

1. **Price anchor is offshore labour, below our floor.** Recurring standardisable B2B tasks on these marketplaces clear at **$2–9/hr** (Tally, QBO, FBA cases, listing reposts) or **$10–60 per deliverable** (UK payroll $15, product upload $60/1,000). A buyer spending $20–150/month on a human cannot also replace spend of **2–5× a €50–200 subscription**. That fails the R3 economics test in `R3-CRITERIA.md` by construction.
2. **When the deliverable is truly standardisable, a $0–60/month self-serve tool already exists.** Examples: Matrixify $20, A2X $29, IncoDocs $0–27, DocuClipper $20, Metricool €16–29, Otterly $29, Fathom $59, HMRC free, Vyapar ₹10k/yr. People still hire humans despite these tools because they are buying **responsibility and hands inside a proprietary system** (K12/K13), not the artifact.
3. **Where frequency is highest (daily/weekly), the work sits inside a platform** the buyer doesn't control: Seller Central, ADP/Paychex, Tally, FB Marketplace (K13/K8).
4. This repeats R2's pattern 3 ("where no cheap tool exists, there is a structural reason") from the demand side.

**Recommendation to the R3 synthesiser:** give little weight to global marketplaces as a source. If this segment is revisited, rerun with a fresh search quota and authenticated/allowed access to Fiverr/Upwork. The most useful checks would be Fiverr per-gig order counts (the "orders in queue" signal) and Upwork "ongoing" posts with >$500/month budgets. Only those can show a spend level of 2–5× our price.
