## 0. Coverage and limits (read first)

- **Searches used:** about 83 successful WebSearch calls, plus 4 that failed on a session rate limit and were re-run after the reset. Around 35 WebFetch calls. The ~150 budget was not used up. I stopped because extra queries kept returning the same vendor pages, not new buyer evidence.
- **Blocked (HTTP 403) on direct fetch:** every Upwork page (job posts, category pages, Project Catalog), every Fiverr gig page, and OnlineJobs.ph job pages. I did not try to get around the blocks.
  - **Upwork evidence is title-level only.** The search engine gives job *titles* and URLs. It almost never gives the budget or hourly range. Wherever this file cites an Upwork post, it proves only that a buyer posted that job. **The budget is UNVERIFIED.**
  - **Fiverr evidence** comes from search-result titles, which include the starting price (for example "for $40").
  - **OnlineJobs.ph evidence** comes from search snippets that quote the salary.
- **Fetchable:** Freelancer.com job lists, PeoplePerHour (PPH) job lists and single posts, vendor pricing pages. As in R3, Freelancer.com recurring work clears at **$5–16/hr** ([VA list](https://www.freelancer.com/jobs/virtual-assistant/), [accounting list](https://www.freelancer.com/jobs/accounting/)). That confirms R3's price anchor again.
- **Budget ≥ $500/month filter:** very few marketplace posts showed a budget at that level. Most spend above $500/month shows up as **specialist done-for-you firms with quote-only pricing**, not as marketplace posts. I therefore used those firms' published or third-party price ranges as the "human provider" price and marked the evidence strength.
- **R3 revival check:** R4's relaxed K12 and replaced K14 could revive R3 marketplace kills. I re-checked the three that were killed mainly on K12/K14: C1 bank reconciliation, C10 FBA reimbursement and C12 export documents. **None revives** (see rows 26–28).

## 2. Per-candidate notes (only what the table does not say)

**#1 Certified payroll.** Contractors on Davis-Bacon and state public-works jobs must submit a certified payroll **every week, for each project** ([DOE/LCPtracker](https://www.energy.gov/infrastructure/weekly-dba-payroll-tracking-lcptracker)). The deliverable is generated from payroll data: WH-347 PDF, or XML for California's DIR eCPR, uploaded to LCPtracker, Elations, eMars or state portals. Two Upwork posts show buyers looking for humans. Several paid DFY firms exist. All of them are **quote- or call-based** (Points North [link](https://www.points-north.com/certified-payroll-reporting), Public Works Consultant [link](https://publicworksconsultant.com/), Payroll4Construction "You submit time. We do the rest" [link](https://www.payroll4construction.com/)). Cheap self-serve software also exists ($19–49/mo). A vendor claims that DIY with software still takes "2 hours/week" ([link](https://www.certifiedpayrollpro.com/certified-payroll-services), UNVERIFIED, competitor marketing). Legal note: the contractor signs the Statement of Compliance, not the preparer ([DOL](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/WHD/prevailing-wage-presentations/DBA_Certified_Payrolls.pdf)), so K12 sign-off does not trigger. Error exposure (back wages, debarment) still makes the buyer care about trust.

**#2 Prequalification-platform upkeep.** ISNetworld has "no API, no automatic data sync" ([BasinCheck](https://basincheck.com/resources/best-software-isnetworld-compliance)). Everything is typed or uploaded by hand. The recurring work: "Some clients require monthly reporting of hours worked on-site, employee headcount, miles driven, incident counts, and subcontractor data—broken out by site location" ([ISI Environmental](https://isienvironmental.com/isnetworld-blog/)). Insurance must be uploaded at each renewal, or grades fail. There are OSHA figures quarterly and yearly, plus new hiring-client requirements. The expensive part (written safety programs for RAVS review) is now sold for $35–149 one-off. What remains paid is the **monthly monitoring retainer at $100–500**. Buyer frustration is documented ("paying ISNetworld, then a company for safety stuff, and then a third party", [PissedConsumer via snippet](https://isn-software.pissedconsumer.com/review.html), weak). Account access for helpers: "no restriction on how many users" (search snippet from ISN pages, UNVERIFIED).

**#3 Legal e-billing.** Insurance carriers impose billing guidelines and portals (Legal-X, CounselLink, Collaborati, Tymetrix). Invoices get rejected or reduced, and someone has to write the appeal. Outsourcers target small and mid-sized firms that work "for insurance carriers, third-party administrators" ([ALB](https://www.accuratelegalbilling.com/e-billing-services)). **No outsourcer publishes a price.** So this candidate fails R4's "every price needs a URL" rule for the DFY price. Only the in-house wage has a URL.

**#4 COI tracking.** The clearest case where **buyers do adopt the tool**. Free tiers plus automated vendor chasing ($0–49/mo) cover small portfolios. Full DFY is priced for mid-market buyers ($10k/yr minimum at BCS). The Upwork coordinator posts are probably mid-size firms, and at that size software-plus-staff incumbents (myCOI, BCS full-service) already compete.

**#5 DQF.** This is already a productised, fixed-price, per-driver DFY market ($30–49/driver), which is exactly the model R4 would build. A late solo entrant has no edge, and the MVR/Clearinghouse data flows are regulated.

**#6 Credentialing.** The maintenance-only slice (re-attest, upload renewed licences) is low-touch. But buyers buy credentialing as a bundle with enrolment, and enrolment needs payer phone follow-up. Therapists, the biggest small-practice segment, get it free on Headway.

**#10 Dental KPI pack.** The best-described buyer post in the sweep: monthly, multi-practice, concrete deliverable, and it names the data sources (Xero + Dentally/SoE/R4). It still dies because the buyer explicitly wants ACCA/CIMA-qualified commentary. Dental accountants already sell fixed monthly fees.

## 3. Deep-dive on the three UNCERTAIN candidates

### 3.1 Certified payroll reports (#1)

1. **Who pays a human today, how much, how often?** Small and mid subcontractors on public works. Evidence: two Upwork buyer posts (budgets UNVERIFIED); DFY from "$90.00 per week" ([link](https://payrollpayroll.com/services/certified-payroll/)) ≈ $390/month; managed "$1,000–5,000/month" (competitor claim, UNVERIFIED); Points North listing $175/mo + $7.50/report + $995 setup (the listing doesn't say whether it is managed or software, [link](https://apps.adp.com/en-us/apps/253943/points-north-certified-payroll-reporting-for-run-powered-by-adp/configure)). Frequency: **weekly, per project**.
2. **Why not the cheap tool?** Weak evidence only. Tools still need someone to map classifications and wage determinations, compute fringes, handle apprentice ratios and log into each agency portal. Vendors describe the job as "a manual nightmare" ([ImageToTable](https://imagetotable.ai/blog/certified-payroll-compliance-manual-problem), vendor content). The **strongest counter-evidence** is K2: construction payroll providers (Miter, Payroll4Construction) generate and export certified payroll automatically. A buyer who dislikes the chore may switch payroll provider instead.
3. **Human time and capacity (estimate, UNVERIFIED).** Assume a customer with 2 active projects, weekly payroll CSV/PDF export sent async.
   - Today's human provider: about 1.5–2 h/week ≈ **7–9 h/month** (by analogy with the vendor's 2 h/week DIY claim).
   - With software (parse payroll export → apply stored per-project classification/rate map → generate WH-347 PDF + DIR XML → human checks exceptions → human uploads to portal): about 45–60 min/week ≈ **3–4 h/month**, plus about 3 h one-time per new project. Leverage ≈ 50–60%. That is borderline on the ≥50% test and depends on how often portal uploads need manual entry rather than an XML/CSV import.
   - Capacity: **5 customers × €200 = €1,000 MRR; 5 × 4 h = 20 h/month ≤ 43 h.** At €300, 4 customers × 4 h = 16 h. It fits, with headroom for new-project setups.
   - Schedule risk: weekly deadlines per customer clash with a 10 h/week side-project cadence. A holiday means missed filings.
4. **Self-serve purchase without a call?** Plausible but unproven: a fixed-price page ("€X per project per week, send your payroll export, get portal-ready reports within 48 h"), checkout, and an async onboarding form (portal logins, wage determinations, classifications). Every DFY competitor found requires a call or quote. That is a gap, but it may exist because buyers *want* the call. Main channel is search for "certified payroll service" plus state-specific pages (CA DIR eCPR, WA L&I, NY). US-only market.

**Adversarial finding:** fixed-price DFY rivals exist (Payroll Service Plus "$90/week"), and cheap software starts at $19/mo. Differentiation would be "no call, fixed price, portal upload included". That is thin, but it's not zero.

### 3.2 Prequalification-platform upkeep (#2)

1. **Who pays, how much, how often?** Small industrial/energy contractors whose clients require ISNetworld/Avetta/Veriforce. Monitoring $100–300/mo basic, $250–500 enhanced ([link](https://www.safetyproresources.com/blog/what-does-it-cost-to-hire-a-good-isnetworld-consultant)). Monthly Site Tracker stats per client site ([link](https://isienvironmental.com/isnetworld-blog/)).
2. **Why not a tool?** No tool can submit into ISN (no API), and the platform fee itself is $875+/yr ([Capterra via snippet](https://www.capterra.com/p/145737/ISNetworld/pricing/)). Buyers here clearly do pay humans.
3. **Human time and capacity (estimate).** Monthly stats entry 0.5–1 h, insurance/OSHA uploads averaging 0.5 h/month, grade checks and deficiency responses 1–2 h. That's roughly **2–4 h/month**, with spikes at renewal. Capacity: 5 × €200 = €1,000, 5 × 3 h = 15 h. It fits.
   - **Leverage test likely fails.** Software can only prepare the data (collect hours from the customer by form, precompute rates, check expiry dates). The portal typing is the same for us and for a human rival, so the cut is probably 20–40%, not ≥50% (UNVERIFIED). Browser automation of ISN is a terms-of-service risk (not checked).
   - **Expertise gap:** RAVS deficiencies need US OSHA/safety-program knowledge the founder does not have.
4. **Self-serve?** Rivals are all call/demo based (EHS Inc., Safety Services Company, IRONWOOD, QuickComply). A checkout-based "ISN monthly upkeep €X" would be unusual. Contractors in this segment are phone-first (UNVERIFIED).

### 3.3 Legal e-billing (#3)

1. **Who pays, how much?** Small insurance-defence firms. The outsourced price is **unknown** (all quote-only). In-house staff earn $21–32/hr. Vendor claim: 15–20 h/month of billing tasks (UNVERIFIED).
2. **Why not the tool?** Law-practice software exports LEDES and checks some guideline rules, but carrier portals, per-carrier guidelines and appeals still need a person. No evidence was found of buyers trying and dropping a tool.
3. **Human time and capacity (estimate).** A firm with 30–60 invoices/month: automated pre-bill scrub, then upload at about 5–10 min/invoice, then about 3–6 appeals at about 20 min each. That's roughly **5–10 h/month**, against 15–20 h today (≈50% leverage, borderline). Capacity: 4 customers × €300 = €1,200, 4 × 8 h = 32 h ≤ 43 h. It fits, barely. The price level is **unverified**.
4. **Self-serve?** Weak. Firms hand over privileged billing narratives and portal logins; a no-call purchase from an unknown solo vendor seems unlikely (UNVERIFIED). Appeals are argument with the carrier, which is close to K12 negotiation.

## 4. Segment finding

1. **Marketplace buyer posts rarely show ≥ $500/month.** Where budgets are visible (Freelancer, PPH, OnlineJobs), recurring back-office work clears at $3.50–16/hr. That is R3's finding again. Upwork might show higher budgets, but budgets are not readable from snippets.
2. **Spend above $500/month sits with *specialist DFY firms that price by quote*,** in regulated, portal-bound niches: certified payroll, prequalification platforms, legal e-billing, credentialing, DOT files. This supports R3 pattern #4 (buyers pay to have responsibility taken off their plate).
3. **Where a niche is fully productised with a public per-unit price** (DQF $30/driver, COI $0.95/vendor, TaxJar $50/return, MarginEdge $350/location), **the market already has the R4 model** and a solo entrant has no edge. The openings are the niches where DFY is still **quote-only** (#1, #2, #3). Their weakness: quote-only may reflect buyers who want a phone relationship, which R4 forbids.
4. **The leverage test is the binding constraint, not capacity.** In portal-bound work without an API, software can prepare the data but a human still types or uploads. Candidates pass the ≥50% cut only where the portal accepts a file upload (certified payroll XML/CSV, LEDES files). That favours #1 and #3 over #2.
5. **No R3 kill is revived** by R4's relaxed K12/K14.
